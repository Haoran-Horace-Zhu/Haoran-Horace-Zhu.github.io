Research

My research connects combinatorics with probability theory and mathematical physics.

Some areas of mathematics no longer excite me as much as they once did. AI is changing the way we work so quickly, and I find it sad to see us becoming more guarded and less trusting of one another.

But I’m still happy to talk about the topics below, so please get in touch if anything catches your interest. I’d also love to explore new directions together, perhaps in a field where theory and experiment go hand in hand.

Combinatorics

Cayley graphs, symmetric functions, quadratic forms, and association schemes.

Probability Theory

Markov chains, random walks, the interchange process, the exclusion process, cutoff phenomena, and random partitions.

Mathematical Physics

Vertex-operator calculus, integrable systems, and the boson–fermion correspondence.

Other interests

Lie algebras, arithmetic schemes, the Hurwitz composition problem, hypermatrices, quantum determinants, random matrices, and quantum information.

  1. Matrices as graded BiHom-algebras and decompositions

    with Jiacheng Sun and Shuanhong Wang

    Quaestiones Mathematicae, 1–24

    DOI arXiv

    We present matrices as graded BiHom-algebras and consider various characteristics of their decompositions. Specifically, we introduce a notion of connection in the support of the grading and use it to construct a family of canonical graded ideals. We show that, under suitable assumptions, such as $\Sigma$-multiplicativity, maximal length, and centre triviality, the matrix BiHom-algebra decomposes into a direct sum of graded simple ideals. We further extend our results to general graded BiHom-algebras over arbitrary base fields. As applications, we reinterpret classical gradings on matrix algebras such as those induced by Pauli matrices and the $\mathbb{Z}_n \times \mathbb{Z}_n$-grading in terms of our setting.

  2. Twisted derivations on arithmetic schemes, Kummer covers, and the Larsson conjecture

    Journal of Number Theory 291, 529–550

    DOI

    We classify Kummer--Witt Hom--Lie algebras attached to affine Kummer algebras $K[z]/(z^n-B)$ with $B\in K^\times$, modelling the étale locus of tame Kummer covers, in terms of the order of the twisting parameter. We obtain a trichotomy: the Hom--Lie algebra is either abelian, non-abelian but two-step solvable, or perfect. As an application, excluding the identity automorphism, we show that Larsson's conjecture holds precisely for even composite $n$ and fails for odd composite $n$. We also give an operator-theoretic interpretation of the Hom--Lie bracket as an order-preserving twisted commutator, and relate its adjoint weights to the $q$-integers occurring in the one-variable $q$-de Rham differential.

  3. Fredholm determinants as $\tau$-functions of the KP hierarchies via $t$-deformed vertex operators

    Physics Letters B 884, 141199

    DOI

    We show that the Fredholm determinants arising from arbitrary multiplicative statistics of the $t$-Schur measure form a family of $\tau$-functions of the 2D Toda hierarchy, with the positive flows naturally dressed by the $t$-deformation, via a vertex-operator realisation of $t$-Schur functions. We also study the dual dressing of the positive flows and obtain the corresponding family of Toda $\tau$-functions. Our construction encompasses the Okounkov–Lazag formulae as a special case when $t=0$.

  1. Diagonal trace identities for bosonic and fermionic matrices

    Theoretical and Mathematical Physics (to appear)

    PDF

    We determine the second stabilised diagonal of finite-rank fermionic trace relations in the $\partial\psi$ model. In this paper, the formal letters of the model are treated as independent odd matrix letters, denoted $\psi_0,\psi_1,\psi_2,\ldots$. For every $N\geqslant 2$, the charge-$2N+2$ relation space at rank $N$ has odd dimension $4$ and even dimension $4$. More precisely, it has a basis consisting of the eight signed polarised Cayley--Hamilton trace identities obtained by inserting copies of the even word $\psi_0^2$. This proves a conjecture of Eleftheriou, Ji, and Murthy.

  2. A note on some spectral properties of generalised pancake graphs

  3. A new PBW basis for the alternating central extension of the $q$-Onsager algebra

    Nuclear Physics B 1029, 117577

    DOI arXiv

    We establish a new PBW basis for $\mathcal A_q$, the alternating central extension of the $q$-Onsager algebra. Terwilliger showed that the alternating generators form a PBW basis in the block order $\mathcal G\lt\mathcal W^-\lt\mathcal W^+\lt\widetilde{\mathcal G}$. We prove that they also form a PBW basis in the different block order $\mathcal W^-\lt\mathcal G\lt\widetilde{\mathcal G}\lt\mathcal W^+$. Consequently, multiplication induces a vector-space isomorphism \[ \mathcal W^-\otimes\mathcal G\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal G}\otimes\mathcal W^+ \longrightarrow \mathcal A_q, \] thereby confirming a conjecture of Terwilliger.

  1. BiHom–Lie brackets and the Toda equation

    with Botong Gai, Chuanzhong Li, Jiacheng Sun, and Shuanhong Wang

    Symmetry 17, 2176

    DOI arXiv

    We introduce a BiHom-type skew-symmetric bracket on $\mathfrak{gl}(V)$ built from two commuting inner automorphisms $\alpha=Ad_\psi$ and $\beta=Ad_\phi$ with $\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)$ and integers $i,j$. We prove that $(\mathfrak{gl}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]^{(i,j)}_{(\psi,\phi)},\alpha,\beta)$ is a BiHom--Lie algebra, and we study the Lax equation obtained by replacing the commutator in the finite nonperiodic Toda lattice by this bracket. For the symmetric choice $\phi=\psi$ with $(i,j)=(0,0)$, the deformed flow is equivariant under conjugation and becomes gauge-equivalent, via $\widetilde L=\psi^{-1}L\psi$, to a Toda-type Lax equation with a conjugated triangular projection. In particular, scalar deformations amount to a constant rescaling of time. On embedded $2\times2$ blocks, we derive explicit trigonometric and hyperbolic formulas that make symmetry constraints (e.g. tracelessness) transparent. In the asymmetric hyperbolic case, we exhibit a trace obstruction showing that the right-hand side is generically not a commutator, which amounts to symmetry breaking of the isospectral property. We further extend the construction to the weakly coupled Toda lattice with an indefinite metric and provide explicit $2\times2$ solutions via an inverse-scattering calculation, clarifying and correcting certain formulas in the literature. The classical Toda dynamics are recovered at special parameter values.

  1. Kronecker coefficients and Harrison centres of the representation ring of the symmetric group

    With Jia-Cheng Sun and Chi Zhang

    2024

    arXiv

    We present a computational approach to studying the structure of the representation ring of the symmetric group in dimension six. The Kronecker coefficients and all power formulae of irreducible representations of $S_6$ are computed using the character theory of finite groups. In addition, considering direct sum decomposition of tensor products of different irreducible representations of $S_6$, we characterise generators of the representation ring $\mathcal{R}(S_6)$, show that its unit group $U(\mathcal{R}(S_6))$ is a Klein four-group, and related results on the structure of primitive idempotents. Furthermore, we introduce the Harrison centre theory to study the representation ring and show that the Harrison centre of the cubic form induced by the generating relations of $\mathcal{R}(S_6)$ is isomorphic to itself. Finally, we conclude with some open problems for future consideration.

    We completed this work in 2023, which was originally written in Chinese and later translated into English. Our initial aim was just to work out the case of $\mathfrak{S}_6$ explicitly. Later we brought in Harrison theory to explore a connection between the Kronecker product and Harrison centres. To be honest, the results can mainly be viewed as elementary computations. Of the two conjectures in the final part of the paper, I think the first is quite trivial and the second should also be easy to prove, although I still have some interest in settling the second.

  2. Spectral gaps of Cayley graphs of $B_n$ generated by all transpositions and a single odd sign flip

    With Gary Greaves

    2025

    PDF

    We construct infinite families of Cayley graphs of the Weyl group of type $B$, where the generating set is a non-negative symmetric linear combination of diagonal flips with odd cardinality and transpositions. We show that, for these graphs, the random walk and the interchange process have different spectral gaps. This construction contradicts a conjecture of Cesi, which is a generalisation of Aldous' spectral gap conjecture.

    I first came across Cesi's conjecture in 2023, but only began working on it in 2024 and early 2025. There were long breaks along the way, and I discussed the problem with Gary Greaves. In September 2025, we found a counterexample for $n=4$. Gary suggested extending it to general $n$, which led to these notes. For general $n$, we compared two $n\times n$ representation Laplacians whose bipartition labels have their two parts interchanged.

    While revising the manuscript, we came across Alon and Ghosh's preprint. Their work gave a more complete treatment of the problem, and we decided not to pursue publication. I am keeping these notes for the explicit calculations, which may be useful to anyone learning how to work out these representation matrices.

  3. Harrison centre invariance of hypermatrix algebras and representation rings

    With Jiacheng Sun, Jiaming Wang, and Chi Zhang

    2025

    PDF

    We establish a hypermatrix algebraic criterion for Harrison centre invariance. Given fusion data, we treat the order-$3$ fusion tensor as a hypermatrix and use its layer matrices to generate a hypermatrix algebra. If the fusion tensor is fully symmetric and the left-regular multiplication matrices are linearly independent, the Harrison centre of the associated cubic form coincides with the hypermatrix algebra. As an application, we prove that the representation ring of the symmetric group is isomorphic to the corresponding Harrison centre.

    This work grew out of the second conjecture in our earlier note on Kronecker coefficients and Harrison centres. Jiaming Wang did most of the work, which also formed the main part of his thesis. The four of us then worked together to put the paper into its present form. We are not planning to submit it for now.

  4. Counting faithful families of integrals in weak algebraic quantum groups

    With Jiacheng Sun and Shuanhong Wang

    2025

    PDF

    We introduce an invariant $N(A)$, defined as the minimal size of a faithful family of left or right integrals for a regular weak multiplier Hopf algebra with sufficiently many integrals. Using the Van Daele–Wang spanning criterion, we characterise $N(A)$ and prove $N(A)\leq 2\dim\varepsilon_t(A)$ when the target algebra $\varepsilon_t(A)$ is finite-dimensional. In the $\ast$-algebraic setting, we show that for positive left integrals, either spanning condition from Van Daele–Wang alone ensures faithfulness, and any finite faithful family of positive left integrals can be merged into one faithful positive integral. Hence, if $\varepsilon_t(A)$ is finite-dimensional and there is a faithful family of positive left integrals, then $N(A)=1$. We compute $N(A)$ for groupoid examples and always find $N(A)=1$. For packed algebras from finite-object Hopf categories, the object count gives only a coarse upper bound, but in the finite-dimensional Frobenius case we again obtain $N(A)=1$. Collectively, these results provide a partial answer to a question posed by Van Daele and Wang.

    We started this work quite some time ago, following a question of Shuanhong Wang. Our main results concern finite-dimensional target algebras, but we would really like to understand the infinite-dimensional case. We have been meaning to return to this, but have not got to it yet, so we are not planning to submit the present version. If you are interested in the question, I would be very happy to explore it together.

  5. A symmetry formula for correlation functions in the superintegrable chiral Potts spin chain

    2026

    arXiv

    We prove an exact finite-volume symmetry formula for two-point functions in the periodic $N$-state superintegrable chiral Potts spin chain. We show that, for every chain length $L$ and every simultaneous eigenvector of the Hamiltonian and the one-site translation operator, the correlations satisfy $\langle Z_0^r Z_R^{\dagger r}\rangle^*=\langle Z_0^r Z_{L-R}^{\dagger r}\rangle$ for $1\leqslant r\leqslant N-1$. Hence, whenever $L$ is even, the midpoint correlation $\langle Z_0^r Z_{L/2}^{\dagger r}\rangle$ is real. Then we generalise the three-state chain case to arbitrary $N$ and to every translation eigensector. This resolves a conjecture of Fabricius and McCoy.

    This grew out of a simple mathematical observation. The write-up does not quite fit the style of a physics paper, and I don’t plan to take it further or seek publication.

  6. Coxeter numbers and spectral gaps of rank-three weighted group-algebra Laplacians

    2026

    PDF

    We construct a positive weight system on the reflections of the Coxeter group $B_3$ for which the spectral gap of the corresponding weighted group-algebra Laplacian is attained in a three-dimensional irreducible representation of generalised Coxeter number $8$, although the Coxeter number of $B_3$ is $6$. In particular, the spectral gap is not always attained in an irreducible representation of generalised Coxeter number equal to the Coxeter number. This gives a negative answer to the first part of Question 9.3 of Chapuy and Douvropoulos.

    These notes grew out of calculations I made in February 2026. I submitted the paper once, but it was not accepted. With AI developing so quickly, I believe it could now do these calculations better than I did, although I have not tried it. I don’t plan to submit it again. The question has something of the spirit of Aldous’s spectral gap conjecture, which I like.

  1. Coxeter groups, irreducible representations, and spectral gap realisations

    Northeastern University, 2024

    PDF

    We determine the spectral gaps of several families of random walks on the Weyl group $D_n$ of type $D_n$ driven by non-negative weights on its reflections, and we identify irreducible representations that attain them. Such weights correspond to weighted signed graphs, and the random walk is an interchange process in which cards may be turned over in pairs. We show that the motion of a single card is governed by the Laplacian of the underlying graph and by the signed Laplacian, which describe the action of the walk on the standard and the reflection representations. For uniform weights, for weights on complete signed graphs that are constant on the positive and on the negative edges, for uniform weights on the reflections moving a fixed coordinate, and for weights taking one value on the reflections of $D_{n-1}$ and another on the remaining reflections of $D_n$, the spectral gap is attained by the reflection representation. For the reflections moving a fixed coordinate it is also attained by the standard representation, which gives a type-$D$ analogue of the theorem of Flatto, Odlyzko and Wales on star transpositions. For $n \geq 5$ we construct strictly positive weights on the reflections of $D_n$ whose spectral gap is attained only by the standard representation, so that neither the standard nor the reflection representation can be omitted. Weights of the same kind, together with analogous constructions in type $B$, give a negative answer to the first part of a question of Chapuy and Douvropoulos on generalised Coxeter numbers for the Weyl groups of types $D_n$ with $n \geq 4$ and $B_n$ with $n \geq 3$. In rank three the problem reduces to the theorem of Caputo, Liggett and Richthammer, whereas in rank four, because of triality, further irreducible representations are needed. On the basis of these results and of numerical computations, we formulate a type-$D$ analogue of Aldous's spectral gap conjecture.
